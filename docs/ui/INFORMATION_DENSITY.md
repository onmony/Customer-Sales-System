# Information Density

Professional users require immediate access to comprehensive data. The system must prioritize information density and clarity over sparse, decorative layouts.

## Core Principles

### Dense but Readable
The interface should present a high volume of information without becoming visually overwhelming. Use typography, alignment, and subtle visual cues to maintain readability in dense data views.

### Useful Information Over Decoration
Every pixel must serve a purpose. Avoid purely aesthetic elements that consume valuable screen real estate without providing actionable business context.

### Business Context First
Group and present data in a way that aligns with the business logic. Related information should be visually connected, allowing the user to grasp the complete context at a glance.

### Professionals Prefer Information
Do not hide crucial data behind clicks, hovers, or "read more" links under the assumption that the screen is "too busy." Professional users are trained to parse dense information quickly; hiding it slows them down.

### Avoid Unnecessary Whitespace
While breathing room is important for visual hierarchy, excessive whitespace reduces the amount of data visible without scrolling. Optimize layouts to maximize data visibility.

### Compress Before Remove
When space is constrained, reduce whitespace before removing useful business information.

Preferred sequence:

1. Compress spacing.
2. Reduce padding.
3. Reduce card height.
4. Collapse optional sections.
5. Resize components.

Only remove information if it has no operational value.

### Avoid Information Overload
Density does not mean chaos. Employ progressive disclosure and logical grouping to ensure that while much information is available, it is structured logically so the user can easily find what they need.

### Most Important Information First
The most critical data points required for decision-making must be the most prominent. Use visual hierarchy (size, weight, placement) to guide the user's eye to the key metrics or status indicators.

### Tables are Primary Productivity Tools
Data grids and tables are the most efficient way to present large sets of structured data. They must be highly functional, supporting sorting, filtering, quick scanning, and efficient keyboard navigation.
